### Safeguarding Policy
**Required**:
- [ ] Written safeguarding policy (attach document)
- [ ] Covers adults at risk AND children (if work with under-18s)
- [ ] Named safeguarding lead
- [ ] Reporting procedures (who do members contact if concerned?)
- [ ] Response procedures (what happens when concern raised?)

**Snippet for applications**:
```

- Clear reporting procedures for concerns about harm or abuse
- Named Safeguarding Lead: [NAME, CONTACT]
- Risk assessments for all activities
- Responses to disclosures (supporting survivors, managing alleged perpetrators)
- Confidentiality and information-sharing protocols

Our policy recognizes the specific vulnerabilities of QTIPOC communities
(racism, homophobia, transphobia, intersection of marginalizations) and
trauma-informed approaches to all interactions.

Policy last reviewed: [DATE]
Next review: [DATE]
```

**Evidence to attach**:

- Safeguarding Lead training certificates (if any)
- Incident log template (showing you have procedures)

## 5. RISK MANAGEMENT & SUSTAINABILITY

### Risk Assessment
**Required for most funders**:
- [ ] What could go wrong? (identify risks)
- [ ] How likely is each risk?
- [ ] What's the impact if it happens?
- [ ] How are you mitigating each risk?

**Common risks for BLKOUT**:

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| **Founder burnout** | High | High | Recruit co-leads, distribute workload, secure funding for paid coordinator |
| **Low member engagement** | Medium | High | Activate underutilized features (courses, hotseats), Community Credits incentives |
| **Safeguarding incident** | Low | Very High | Clear safeguarding policy, trained moderators, incident response plan |
| **Data breach** | Low | High | Encrypted storage, secure platforms (Heartbeat.chat), data minimization |
| **Harassment/abuse in community** | Medium | High | Community Guidelines, moderation team, restorative justice processes |
| **Funding dependency** | High | Medium | Build earned income (cooperative memberships), diversify funding sources |
| **Platform failure (Heartbeat.chat)** | Low | Medium | Data export/backup, migration plan to alternative platforms |
| **Volunteer turnover** | Medium | Medium | Community Credits recognition, clear roles, succession planning |
| **Scope creep/mission drift** | Medium | Medium | Regular strategy reviews, member input on priorities, say no to off-mission work |

**Snippet for applications**:
```
We have identified key organizational risks and put mitigation strategies
in place:

[Select 3-5 most relevant risks from table above, describe mitigations]

Risk register reviewed quarterly by board. All major decisions include
risk assessment.
```

## 7. FUNDER-SPECIFIC DUE DILIGENCE

Different funders emphasize different things:

### Charitable foundations often want:
- Charity registration (may not fund unregistered)
- 3+ independent trustees (not family/friends)
- Annual accounts (2 years minimum)
- Reserves policy
- Child protection policy (even if don't work with children - shows diligence)

### Government/public funders often want:
- Full legal compliance documentation
- Insurance certificates
- Equality impact assessments
- Value for money evidence
- Monitoring & evaluation frameworks

### LGBT+/identity-specific funders often want:
- Lived experience leadership (board/staff who are LGBT+/QTIPOC)
- Community accountability (how do beneficiaries shape your work?)
- Intersectional analysis (not single-issue)
- Trauma-informed approaches

### Racial justice funders often want:
- Black-led/POC-led governance
- Structural racism analysis (not just diversity/representation)
- Community organizing approach (not charity/service delivery)
- Solidarity with other movements

### Tech/innovation funders often want:
- Clear theory of change (how tech creates social impact)
- Open source commitment (or explanation why proprietary)
- Accessibility plans (tech for all, not just tech-savvy)
- Data ethics (how you handle sensitive information)

## 8. RED FLAGS TO AVOID

**Things that make funders nervous**:

❌ **No written policies** (looks unprofessional/risky)
❌ **Single founder/leader** (what if they leave? burnout risk?)
❌ **No financial oversight** (one person controls all money)
❌ **No safeguarding measures** (especially for vulnerable communities)
❌ **Mission creep** (trying to do too much, unclear focus)
❌ **Unrealistic budgets** (either too high or suspiciously low)
❌ **No track record** (never delivered anything before)
❌ **Poor references** (people can't vouch for you)
❌ **Defensive attitude** (won't accept feedback, sees funders as adversaries)
❌ **No evaluation plan** (how will you know if it worked?)

**How to address if you have red flags**:

**If you're a new organization**:
```
While BLKOUT is a young organization ([X years old]), our leadership
brings [X years combined experience] in [community organizing/QTIPOC
advocacy/etc.]. We have [piloted programs, built partnerships, engaged
X members] demonstrating our capacity to deliver. We are actively
building governance structures including [writing policies, recruiting
board members, securing insurance] and welcome funder guidance on
strengthening our operations.
```

**If you're founder-led/small team**:
```
BLKOUT is currently founder-led, reflecting the grassroots origins of
our work. We recognize succession planning and distributed leadership
as priorities. This grant will enable us to [recruit co-leads, pay
coordinator, formalize governance] reducing dependency on single
individuals. We are actively recruiting [X] additional board members
with skills in [finance, legal, tech, organizing].
```

**If you lack policies**:
```
We are in process of formalizing organizational policies. [X policies]
are complete and attached. [Y policies] are in draft and will be
finalized by [DATE]. We have budgeted £[X] for legal/governance support
to ensure all policies meet best practice standards.
```

## 9. DUE DILIGENCE TIMELINE

**Don't apply for funding until you can answer these questions**:

**BEFORE ANY APPLICATION** (Do once, use forever):
- [ ] Confirm legal structure and registration
- [ ] Write Constitution/Articles (if don't have)
- [ ] Recruit minimum 3 trustees/directors (if don't have)
- [ ] Set up bank account with dual authorization
- [ ] Draft Safeguarding Policy
- [ ] Draft Data Protection Policy
- [ ] Draft Community Guidelines
- [ ] Create budget template
- [ ] Gather 3-5 member testimonials
- [ ] Identify 2-3 referees
- [ ] Create impact tracking system (even simple spreadsheet)

**TIMELINE**:
- Week 1: Audit current status (what do you have? what's missing?)
- Week 2-3: Write missing policies (use templates, adapt to BLKOUT)
- Week 4: Review with board/members, finalize
- Week 5+: Ready to apply for funding

**Don't let perfect be enemy of good**:
- Policies can be 2-3 pages, not 30-page manuals
- Use free templates (NCVO, ACEVO, Charity Commission websites)
- "In development" is acceptable if you have timeline to complete

## 10. CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

**After each application** (successful or not):

- [ ] Request funder feedback (what was strong? what was weak?)
- [ ] Update policies based on learning
- [ ] Add new testimonials/evidence to library
- [ ] Strengthen weak areas identified
- [ ] Document lessons learned

**Example feedback loop**:
```
LGBT+ Futures feedback: "Your safeguarding policy was strong, but we'd
like to see more detail on how you support members experiencing crisis."

ACTION: Add Crisis Support Protocol to Safeguarding Policy:
- 24-hour crisis line (Samaritans, LGBT+ helplines)
- Member buddy system (peer support)
- Emergency fund (small grants for members in hardship)
- Signposting to specialist services

RESULT: Policy strengthened for all future applications.
```
